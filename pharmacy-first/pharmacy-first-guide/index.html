What the service helps with

What Pharmacy First is

Pharmacy First is an NHS advanced service in England that allows patients to access assessment and treatment for seven common conditions directly from a participating community pharmacy, without needing a GP appointment first. Trained pharmacists use approved Patient Group Directions (PGDs) and clinical assessment tools to decide whether treatment can be supplied in Reliable Direct Pharmacy, whether the patient should be referred to a GP, or whether urgent or emergency care is needed.

The service covers: acute sore throat (including suspected streptococcal infection), acute otitis media (earache) in children and young people, impetigo, infected insect bites and stings, shingles, acute sinusitis, and uncomplicated urinary tract infection (UTI) in women aged 16–64. Consultations take place in a private consultation room. Where treatment is appropriate, the pharmacist can supply prescription-only medicines (including antibiotics where indicated by pathway) at NHS expense, with no prescription charge for eligible NHS patients.

Pharmacy First sits alongside other NHS community pharmacy services alongside other NHS community pharmacy services and is designed to reduce pressure on general practice and urgent care while keeping care close to where people live.

Why it exists

Many presentations to GP surgeries and minor injury units are for self-limiting or straightforward infections that can be safely assessed and treated in primary care with the right clinical tools. Pharmacy First formalises pharmacist-led assessment so patients receive timely treatment, appropriate antibiotic stewardship is applied via pathway criteria, and general practice capacity is preserved for more complex care.

Who should consider this service

2.1 Acute Sore Throat (including suspected streptococcal infection)

Acute sore throat is inflammation of the pharynx, usually viral. A subset is caused by Group A Streptococcus (GAS), which may benefit from antibiotic treatment to shorten symptoms and reduce complications in selected patients. Pharmacy First uses clinical scoring (e.g. FeverPAIN or Centor-style criteria within the NHS pathway) to identify patients likely to have bacterial infection who may be offered antibiotics.

Pain on swallowing, red or swollen throat, fever, absence of cough (more suggestive of GAS), swollen tender anterior cervical lymph nodes, tonsillar exudate or swelling, general malaise, headache.

Adults and children aged 5 years and over with acute sore throat symptoms starting within the last few days, where the patient can be assessed in pharmacy and meets pathway inclusion criteria. Pharmacist completes scoring, excludes red flags, and may supply analgesia and/or antibiotics per PGD if criteria are met.

Symptoms persisting beyond expected recovery despite appropriate treatment, recurrent severe sore throats suggesting need for further investigation, suspected glandular fever or other non-pathway diagnosis, immunocompromised patients outside PGD scope, children under 5, patients who fail to improve within safety-netting timeframe, suspected quinsy (peritonsillar abscess) or other complications.

Severe difficulty breathing or swallowing, drooling, stridor, rapidly worsening throat swelling, severe dehydration, signs of sepsis, airway compromise, unwell immunocompromised patient with systemic features.

What happens during your check

Before appointment

  • Patient identifies symptoms matching one of the seven conditions.
  • Contact participating pharmacy by phone, walk-in, or online booking where available.
  • Bring NHS number if known, list of current medicines, allergy information, and details of symptom onset.
  • For UTI pathway: be ready to provide urine sample if pharmacy requests dipstick testing.
  • For children: parent/carer must attend with child.

During appointment

  • Patient checked in at pharmacy counter; directed to private consultation room.
  • Pharmacist confirms consent, identity, and presenting complaint.
  • Confidential discussion of symptoms, medical history, red-flag screening, and medicines/allergies.
  • Physical assessment as required (throat examination, otoscopy if available, skin inspection, shingles rash review, sinus symptom checklist, urinalysis for UTI).
  • Outcome explained clearly: treat in pharmacy, self-care only, or refer.

Assessment process

  • Structured questions from NHS clinical pathway checklist.
  • Scoring tools where applicable (e.g. sore throat FeverPAIN/Centor elements).
  • Exclusion of referral and emergency criteria documented.
  • Decision recorded in pharmacy clinical system for NHS reporting.
  • Patient verbal consent for any treatment supplied under PGD.

At Reliable Direct Pharmacy, the consultation follows a structured process — not rushed counter advice. The pharmacist explains each step before the assessment begins.
